[1/7]  (279 chars)
  image: fig1 (the cliff) -- carries the whole point, do not post without it
--------------------------------------
Splice an INCORRECT chain of thought into a model's reasoning and let it keep going: can it still reach the right answer in k tries? A pilot on a matched Qwen3-4B thinking/instruct pair.

Short answer: yes, right up until the wrong reasoning is finished.

https://alvinzh04.github.io/blog/completeness-cliff.html

[2/7]  (276 chars)
  image: none (let the numbers stand)
--------------------------------------
Through 25/50/75% of a model's OWN wrong derivation, pass@16 on AIME barely moves: 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 0.91.

At 100% it falls to 0.04, which on this subset is ONE question out of 23. Forcing an instant answer gives the same 0.04, so the open thinking budget buys nothing there.

[3/7]  (276 chars)
  image: fig2 (who spends the open block)
--------------------------------------
Is it just having a thinking mode? Partly.

Handed a complete solution to a DIFFERENT question, only the reasoning-trained model starts over: +0.41 open-block premium vs +0.01 for its instruct sibling, +0.07 for Gemma.

On a generic pseudo-rationale, instruct gains +0.44 too.

[4/7]  (261 chars)
  image: fig4 (the ordering across kinds of wrongness)
--------------------------------------
Recovery (pass@1) by kind of wrongness, same 23 questions:

correct chain w/ corrupted numbers 0.89
generic pseudo-rationale 0.51
complete foreign rationale ~0.38
the model's OWN complete wrong solution 0.02

Its own finished reasoning is in a class of its own.

[5/7]  (258 chars)
  image: fig5 (the paired ablation) -- the methodological core of the post
--------------------------------------
Those bars don't hold the wrong answer fixed, so I ran one that does. Same wrong answer, four ways, recovery:

bare claim 0.33
generic rationale 0.39
short task-spec 0.53
its own complete derivation 0.03

No scaffold explains the collapse. Completeness does.

[6/7]  (269 chars)
  image: none
--------------------------------------
If completion is what closes the argument, doubt should reopen it.

Appending "Wait, let me double-check that." to the finished wrong chain roughly triples pass@16 (0.07 to 0.20 thinking, 0.02 to 0.20 instruct). A neutral "So," does nothing.

Partial rescue, not a fix.

DRAFT A: leads with the number (tends to travel furthest)  (277 chars)
  image: fig1
--------------------------------------
Feed a reasoning model 75% of its own WRONG derivation and it still solves the AIME question within 16 tries (pass@16 0.91). Feed it the whole wrong trace and recovery falls to 0.04: one question out of 23.

Completion, not correctness, is what sticks.

https://alvinzh04.github.io/blog/completeness-cliff.html

DRAFT B: leads with the idea  (257 chars)
  image: fig1
--------------------------------------
A model treats an unfinished rationale as a problem to work and a finished one as a conclusion to state. What flips it is the reasoning looking complete and well-formed, not it being right.

Pilot on injected wrong chains of thought:
https://alvinzh04.github.io/blog/completeness-cliff.html

DRAFT C: leads with the intervention  (233 chars)
  image: fig5
--------------------------------------
Give a model its own finished wrong derivation and it almost never escapes.

Append "Wait, let me double-check that." and pass@16 roughly triples, 0.07 to 0.20. The ability was there; it just wasn't deployed.

https://alvinzh04.github.io/blog/completeness-cliff.html
