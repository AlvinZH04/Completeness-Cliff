Hi Daniel, following our last discussion I had done the following:

I set up the full experimental pipeline for the wrong-reasoning question we discussed:
given a model (instruct or reasoning), if we force it to condition on an INCORRECT
thinking trace (a prefix or the complete rollout, injected inside its think block),
can it still answer correctly among k sampled attempts? I ran the pilot on a matched
model pair (Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 vs Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) plus Gemma-4-E2B as a
cross-family check, on AIME 2024+25 and reasoning-gym puzzles (maze, sudoku), with
N=16 samples per question per condition, about 75k rollouts total on our 2 H100s.

The pipeline (Figure 0): baseline rollouts with each model's card-recommended sampling
params -> harvest the model's own wrong solutions as the injection material -> re-prompt
with wrong traces spliced into the think channel, varying (a) how much of the trace is
injected (25/50/75/100%), (b) whether the model may keep thinking afterward or must
answer immediately, and (c) what KIND of wrongness it is (own wrong solution, another
question's solution, another DOMAIN's solution, a bare wrong claim, a corrupted correct
solution) -> grade only the generated tokens (math_verify / task verifiers) -> pass@k,
plus an "adoption" metric (did it echo the trace's answer?) and a token-level audit of
every continuation.

Main findings:

1. The completeness cliff (Figure 1, the headline). Partial wrong prefixes only reshape
   the answer distribution: through 75% of its own wrong reasoning the thinking model
   still recovers within 16 samples essentially always (pass@16 = 1.00 -> 0.91). But
   given 100% of the wrong trace, with unlimited room to keep thinking, pass@16
   collapses to 0.04, identical to the instruct model and identical to forcing an
   immediate answer. Reasoning training delays the cliff; it does not remove it.
   (Five replications across both model families.)

2. The right to keep thinking is only worth something to trained models, and never on
   their own finished reasoning (Figure 2). The open-vs-forced-answer gain is +0.86 for
   the thinking model on bare wrong assertions and +0.36 on irrelevant traces, but
   ~0.02 on its own complete wrong solution; the instruct model gains ~0 everywhere.
   Gemma generates thought channels but gains ~nothing from them being open, so this
   re-examination disposition comes from reasoning-RL training, not from having a
   thinking mode.

3. Recovery tracks how much re-thinking the injection provokes (Figure 3). Foreign
   traces trigger ~20k tokens of re-derivation; the model's own completed reasoning
   triggers ~1k: it often literally writes "wait" and then concludes anyway.
   Seeing is not doubting.

4. The reasoning scaffold persuades, not the conclusion (Figure 4). The same wrong
   answer is rejected when asserted without steps (recovery 0.51) but accepted when
   wrapped in the model's own plausible derivation (0.02). Arithmetic errors inside a
   correct scaffold are silently repaired (0.89). A complete solution to a different
   problem sits in between, and blatant domain mismatch (a maze trace inside an AIME
   question) is escaped far more often than subtle mismatch: recovery is gated on
   mismatch detection, not correctness checking.

5. Anatomy of the rare escapes: every success under a complete own-wrong trace required
   an external anchor. Either one question where the model's prior is near-certain (15/16
   baseline; possibly memorized) and the trace itself hedges, or one where a cheap
   checkable certificate (a subset sum) catches the error during the model's habitual
   verification section. Never unprompted distrust of the chain itself.

Caveats: 4B-class models only, two families / one RL recipe, model-generated wrong
traces; a few artifact cells are excluded and documented in the report.

Proposed next steps (in priority order): (1) a "Wait,"-append intervention after
complete wrong traces, a one-token test of the commitment interpretation; (2) a scale
sweep (9B/14B/30B); (3) an R1-distill replication to test whether the recovery
disposition is training-recipe-general; (4) LLM-written reasoning-error corruption.
Each is <=2 GPU-days with the existing pipeline.

Everything is reproducible from the repo (docs/report_pilot1.md has the full write-up,
